================================================================================
                              IPO-WATCH — PROJECT PLAN
================================================================================

GOAL
----
1. Watch SEC EDGAR for new and updated IPO filings.
2. Watch prediction markets, news and social media for signs of upcoming IPOs.
3. Predict, and keep updating as new information arrives:
     a) the final offer price, compared with the filed price range
     b) how the stock trades in its first week (day 1 "pop", then days 2-5)
   using deal, market, economic, political, geopolitical and social factors.

This is a research and analysis tool, not financial advice.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HOW OFTEN TO CHECK FOR UPDATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Checking every 4 hours is too slow around pricing day. The key filings (price
range set, price range changed, final prospectus) can come in hours apart, and
the market reacts the same day. Checking EDGAR's RSS feed is cheap (one small
request), so the schedule can be tiered:

  Source                        Market hours (ET)   Off-hours / weekends
  ----------------------------  ------------------  --------------------
  EDGAR RSS (new filings)       every 10 min        every 1 hour
  EDGAR full-text search        every 1 hour        every 4 hours
  Prediction markets            every 30 min        every 2 hours
  News / social                 every 30 min        every 2 hours
  Macro / market data           once daily + live*  once daily
  Deals in their pricing week   every 5-10 min      -

  * Live market data (VIX, Nasdaq, IPO ETF) only for deals pricing that day.

SEC rules: at most 10 requests/second, and every request must send a
User-Agent header containing a name and email.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA SOURCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. SEC EDGAR (main source, free)
   Filing forms that matter:
     S-1 / F-1         first IPO registration (F-1 = foreign company)
     S-1/A / F-1/A     amendments; the one that first shows a price range is
                       the key moment
     DRS               confidential draft (shows up only once made public)
     424B4             final prospectus with the final price; the deal has
                       priced
     RW                registration withdrawn; IPO cancelled or postponed
     EFFECT            SEC declared the registration effective
     8-A12B            listing on an exchange; trading is close
   Access:
     - RSS feed of the latest filings (browse-edgar?action=getcurrent)
     - data.sec.gov submissions JSON for each company (CIK)
     - EDGAR full-text search (efts.sec.gov) for things like "initial public
       offering"
   What to pull out of each filing: price range, number of shares, primary vs
   secondary shares, underwriters, exchange and ticker, lock-up terms,
   revenue, net income and growth, use of proceeds, sector (SIC code), VC/PE
   backing, dual-class shares.

B. Prediction markets
   - Kalshi (public API): markets on IPO timing and on market cap / valuation
   - Polymarket (Gamma API): "Will X IPO by <date>?" style markets
   - Manifold (open API): broader coverage but thinner trading
   How they're used: market probabilities are both an early warning and an
   input to the model. The model also tracks how fast the odds change.

C. News and social media
   - News: GDELT (free, global, includes event tone), NewsAPI or Finnhub news,
     Reuters/Bloomberg headlines through RSS where allowed
   - Reddit API: r/stocks, r/wallstreetbets, r/IPO, r/investing
   - StockTwits (by ticker once the ticker is known)
   - Hacker News (Algolia API): useful for tech IPOs
   - X/Twitter: optional because the API is expensive; add later if worth it
   - Google Trends (pytrends): how much retail attention the company gets
   Processing:
     - match each mention to a company or deal (name, ticker, aliases)
     - score sentiment with FinBERT or an LLM classifier
     - measure volume and how fast it's growing

D. Market, economic, political and geopolitical data
   - Market: VIX, S&P 500 / Nasdaq returns over 5/30 days, Renaissance IPO
     ETF (IPO), sector ETFs, recent IPO returns (the "IPO window"), how many
     IPOs are pricing that week
   - Economy (FRED API): Fed funds rate, 10-year yield, yield curve, credit
     spreads, CPI surprise, unemployment, days until the next FOMC meeting
   - Policy and politics: Economic Policy Uncertainty index, government
     shutdown flag (the SEC stops reviewing filings during a shutdown), sector
     regulation news (antitrust, crypto, AI, China ADR rules)
   - Geopolitics: GDELT conflict and tension indices, Geopolitical Risk index
     (Caldara-Iacoviello), tariff and sanctions news, extra flag for
     China-linked issuers
   - Comparable companies: valuations (EV/Revenue) and recent returns of
     listed peers, via yfinance or Financial Modeling Prep

E. Historical training data
   - Jay Ritter's IPO dataset (Univ. of Florida): first-day returns,
     underwriter rankings, VC backing; 1980 to present
   - EDGAR 424B4 + S-1/A history: filed price ranges vs final prices
   - Price history (yfinance / Polygon / Alpaca): opening price, close and
     volume for days 1-5 after the IPO
   Goal: about 10-15 years of US IPOs (around 2,000+ deals). Leave out SPACs
   and direct listings, or label them separately.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT THE MODEL PREDICTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each IPO the model outputs ranges with probabilities, not single numbers:

  T1  where the final price lands vs the range: below / within / above, and
      the expected price as a % of the range midpoint
  T2  day 1 opening "pop" = (first trade price - offer price) / offer price
  T3  day 1 close return vs offer price
  T4  day 5 close return vs offer price, and vs day 1 close
  T5  how volatile it will be in week 1 (the daily trading range)

Each prediction gives a median plus a 10%-90% range, for example:
"Day 1 close: +18% (likely between -5% and +55%)".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FEATURES (MODEL INPUTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deal         amount raised, % of shares floated, width of the price range,
             any range revision and its direction (a strong signal),
             underwriter rank, number of bookrunners, VC/PE backed, primary
             vs secondary shares, dual-class, lock-up length, exchange
Company      sector, age, revenue growth, profitable or not, gross margin,
             valuation vs peers at the range midpoint
Market       VIX level and change, Nasdaq 30-day return, sector ETF momentum,
             average return of the last 10 IPOs, IPO count that week
Economy      rate level and trend, yield curve, credit spreads, days to FOMC
Political    policy uncertainty index, shutdown flag, sector regulation
             sentiment score, election-period flag
Geopolitical geopolitical risk index, conflict tone, tariff and sanctions
             news for the sector or issuer's country, China/foreign issuer
             flag
Social       mention volume and its growth, sentiment average and spread,
             Google Trends, Reddit/StockTwits activity, brand recognition
             (consumer vs B2B)
Prediction   probability that the IPO happens and how fast it's changing,
markets      implied valuation if one exists

Leakage rule: every feature must be stored with the time it became known.
Training only uses what was known at the moment the prediction would have
been made: when the range was set, on pricing night, and at the open on
day 1.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. MODELING APPROACH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1  Baselines: the historical average pop by sector and market
        conditions, plus a simple rule: "priced above range -> big pop".
Step 2  Main model: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM/XGBoost) trained for
        several percentiles at once, giving the 10/50/90% range. They work
        well on mixed tabular data with a few thousand rows.
Step 3  Explainability: SHAP values per prediction, so each forecast says
        why (for example "range raised +15%, hot sector, low VIX").
Step 4  Updating as news arrives: re-score the deal at every event (new
        filing, range revision, sentiment spike, market move). Show how the
        forecast changed over time on a timeline. Optionally, blend in a
        simple Bayesian adjustment that weights recent news.
Step 5  Week-1 path: day 5 is predicted given the day 1 result. Once day 1
        actually trades, plug in the real numbers and re-forecast days 2-5.

Evaluation:
  - Train on the past, test on later years (no random shuffling). For
    example: train 2012-2020, validate 2021-2022, test 2023-present.
  - Metrics: average error on returns, accuracy of the below/within/above
    prediction, whether 80% of outcomes really fall in the 10-90% ranges,
    and the profit/loss of a simple strategy as a sanity check.
  - Check the model across market regimes (2021 boom vs 2022 freeze).

6. ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Language: Python 3.12

ipo_watch/
  config.py            settings, API keys (.env), polling schedule
  db/                  SQLAlchemy models + migrations (SQLite to start,
                       Postgres later)
  collectors/
    edgar.py           RSS + submissions + full-text search, rate-limited
    edgar_parser.py    pull price range, shares, underwriters, financials
                       out of S-1/424B4 documents
    prediction_mkts.py Kalshi, Polymarket, Manifold
    news.py            GDELT, NewsAPI/Finnhub
    social.py          Reddit, StockTwits, HN, Google Trends
    macro.py           FRED, VIX, indices, EPU, GPR
    market_prices.py   peer valuations, IPO price history after listing
  entity/              match mentions and filings to a single "Deal" record
  features/            build features with timestamps (prevents leakage)
  models/
    train.py, predict.py, evaluate.py, explain.py
  scheduler.py         APScheduler jobs on the schedule in section 1
  alerts.py            notify on new S-1, range revision, pricing, big
                       forecast change (email / Discord / Telegram)
  api/ + dashboard     FastAPI + simple web UI: deal list, forecasts,
                       forecast timeline, SHAP explanations
tests/                 pytest; runs automatically after every edit

Main database tables:
  companies, deals, filings, filing_extracts, mentions, sentiment_scores,
  prediction_markets, macro_series, feature_snapshots, predictions,
  outcomes

Deployment: start on a laptop (cron/APScheduler). Later move to a small
cloud VM or a container on a schedule.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. BUILD PHASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 1  Foundation (week 1)
  - Project setup, config, database schema, test setup
  - EDGAR collector: RSS polling + IPO form filter + store filings
  - Alert when a new S-1/F-1/424B4/RW appears
  Done when: running it lists today's real IPO filings and alerts on new ones

Phase 2  Filing parsing (weeks 2-3)
  - Pull price range, shares, underwriters and financials from S-1/A and
    424B4
  - Deal timeline: filed -> range set -> revised -> priced -> trading
  Done when: correct fields for 20 recent IPOs, checked by hand

Phase 3  Historical dataset (weeks 3-4)
  - Load Ritter data + EDGAR history + day 1-5 prices
  - Rebuild the feature snapshots that existed at each point in time
  Done when: a clean training table with roughly 2,000 deals

Phase 4  First model (weeks 5-6)
  - Baselines -> LightGBM percentile models for T1-T4
  - Train-on-past/test-on-future evaluation + SHAP
  Done when: beats the baseline on the test years with ranges that hold up

Phase 5  Outside signals (weeks 6-8)
  - Prediction markets, news, social, macro/political/geopolitical data
  - Sentiment scoring + matching mentions to deals
  - Retrain; keep only the features that clearly help
  Done when: each signal family shows a measured improvement, or is dropped

Phase 6  Live forecasting (weeks 8-9)
  - Scheduler on the tiered schedule, re-score on events, forecast history
  - Dashboard + alerts
  Done when: the system tracks and forecasts live IPOs on its own

Phase 7  Ongoing
  - After each IPO's first week, log what actually happened and compare to
    the forecast
  - Retrain monthly, watch for the model drifting, add new data sources


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. RISKS & NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Small dataset: only a few hundred IPOs a year, and the market changes a
  lot between years. Use few features, regularize, and keep the ranges
  honest.
- Survivorship bias: keep withdrawn and postponed deals in the dataset.
- Social and prediction market data only goes back a few years, so the
  model must work when those values are missing.
- API costs and terms: X/Twitter, Bloomberg and some news APIs are paid or
  restricted. Start with free sources. Follow SEC fair-access rules.
- Day 1 depends heavily on order-book demand, which isn't public. The range
  revision and the final price relative to the range are the best stand-ins.
- Not investment advice. Treat the output as estimates with uncertainty.
